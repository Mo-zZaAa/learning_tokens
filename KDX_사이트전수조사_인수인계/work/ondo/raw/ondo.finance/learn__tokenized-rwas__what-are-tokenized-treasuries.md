# What Are Tokenized Treasuries?

Last Updated: Jun 30

5 min read

Share

[그림: Tokenized Treasuries]

Tokenized RWAs

Key Takeaways

* Tokenized U.S. Treasuries are blockchain-based representations of U.S. government debt instruments. These tokens provide holders with yield from Treasury bills, notes, or Treasury-focused ETFs.
* Blockchain removes traditional barriers that have historically limited access to high-quality yield.
* Tokenized Treasuries represent over $14 billion in onchain value, establishing themselves as one of the most successful real-world asset implementations.
* Ondo Finance, BlackRock, and Franklin Templeton have all launched Treasury tokenization products.
* Implementations vary across legal frameworks, accessibility, and token utility.

U.S. Treasury securities, backed by the full faith and credit of the U.S. government, anchor global finance. There is over $20 trillion in Treasuries outstanding and their yield defines the "risk-free rate" that underpins nearly every financial valuation worldwide.

Yet traditional Treasury access requires establishing accounts with brokers or banks, each with distinct onboarding and compliance requirements. Once purchased, trades typically settle T+1 during market hours, and transferring Treasuries or using them as collateral is impractical, especially for retail users. For international retail investors, barriers are more pronounced, with local broker-dealers imposing custody fees, higher minimums, and additional intermediation layers.

This accessibility gap created an opportunity. What if Treasury yields could become more accessible than at any point in history? What if positions could move instantly between parties without relying on intermediaries? What if Treasury holdings could serve as collateral across platforms?

Tokenization makes these possibilities real, transforming the world's safest asset class into programmable building blocks for global finance.

> "Stablecoins brought dollars onchain, but they left yield on the table. Tokenized Treasuries complete the picture by making productive capital accessible to anyone with an internet connection." — Nathan Allman, Founder of Ondo Finance

## **Tokenized U.S. Treasuries vs Stablecoins**

Both stablecoins and tokenized Treasuries provide dollar-denominated exposure onchain, but they serve distinct economic functions.

Stablecoins are transactional instruments designed to maintain stable value for payments and trading. The GENIUS Act (enacted July 2025) legally prohibits compliant stablecoins from distributing yield to holders.

Tokenized Treasuries, however, distribute the yield generated by underlying government securities to tokenholders. Quasi-permissionless products enable peer-to-peer transactions and DeFi integration, delivering stablecoin-like functionality plus yield. Permissioned products serve qualified investors, functioning more as digital fund shares than transaction-ready tokens.

The practical difference: holding $10,000 in stablecoins generates zero return, while holding $10,000 in tokenized Treasuries earns hundreds of dollars per year.

## **Yield Distribution**

With accumulating tokens like [USDY](https://ondo.finance/usdy) and [OUSG](https://ondo.finance/ousg), yield is reflected through a rising token price while token balances stay constant. For example, 1,000 tokens at $1.00 each start at $1,000, and after one year at 4% yield, those same 1,000 tokens would be worth about $1,040 at roughly $1.04 per token.

## **Types of Tokenized Treasury Products**

The tokenized Treasury market has developed along two distinct paths, each serving different needs.

#### Institutional-Focused Treasury Tokens

Products like Ondo Finance's OUSG and BlackRock's BUIDL are only available to qualified institutional investors through traditional fund structures. These function as digital representations of well-understood investment vehicles, restricted to Qualified Purchasers or similar high-net-worth categories, with transfer restrictions and elevated minimum investments typical of institutional products.

#### Retail-Focused Treasury Tokens

Accessible Treasury tokens bring government-backed yields to global retail investors with fewer accreditation requirements. Tokens can be easily transferred between wallets and integrated with DeFi protocols.Ondo Finance's USDY has pioneered this category, combining stablecoin-like transferability with Treasury yields for non-U.S. audiences.

## **Benefits of Tokenized Treasuries**

Well-designed tokenized Treasuries deliver several core benefits:

#### 1. Income Generation

Unlike stablecoins, which do not pass on income to holders, tokenized Treasuries distribute yield directly to tokenholders, allowing them to earn the risk-free rate onchain.

#### 2. Global Accessibility

Traditional access to Treasury bills requires domestic brokerage accounts, meeting investment minimums, and navigating cross-border restrictions. Tokenized Treasuries significantly reduce these barriers. Users with internet access and a blockchain wallet can participate in what was previously limited to those with access to traditional financial infrastructure.

#### 3. 24/7 Transferability and Instant Settlement

While traditional Treasury markets require T+1 settlement, some tokenized Treasuries like USDY enable instant peer-to-peer transfers 24/7. Some implementations, like USDY and OUSG, have additional infrastructure for instant minting and redemption even outside traditional market hours (subject to daily capacity limits).

#### 4. Composability

Traditional Treasury positions are siloed in separate accounts and difficult to use as collateral across different platforms. Onchain, tokenized Treasuries become programmable building blocks that integrate seamlessly across DeFi, serving as collateral in lending protocols like Flux Finance, deployed in liquidity pools, and more.

#### 5. Transparency

Traditional Treasury funds report holdings quarterly, often weeks after the period ends. Tokenization creates unprecedented transparency through blockchain's public ledger. Every token issuance, transfer, and redemption is recorded onchain with cryptographic certainty, creating an immutable audit trail visible to anyone.

## **Why Tokenized Treasury Implementation Design Matters**

While billions are flowing into tokenized Treasuries, not all implementations are equal. The label "tokenized Treasury" reveals little about whether an instrument provides strong investor protections or genuine accessibility and seamless composability.

Two critical dimensions help determine whether a tokenized Treasury delivers on tokenization's promise.

### Legal Structures and Investor Protections

Tokenized Treasuries exist at the intersection of blockchain technology and securities law. Strong implementations include multiple layers of protection such as:

* **Bankruptcy-remote structures**: Special purpose vehicles designed to legally separate token-backing assets from parent company operations.
* **Perfected security interests**: Public filings establishing tokenholders' priority claim on collateral. This legal "perfection" ensures backing assets aren't promised to multiple parties.
* **Independent verification**: Third-party agents who regularly verify backing assets, providing transparency beyond issuer claims.
* **Collateral agents**: Independent parties with contractual authority to seize and distribute assets to tokenholders if default events occur.
* **Clear redemption rights**: Contractually specified procedures for converting tokens back to dollars or underlying assets, with enforceable mechanisms rather than issuer discretion.

### Accessibility and Transferability

Tokenized Treasuries vary dramatically in who can access them and how they function:

* **Permissioned models**: Products like OUSG (Ondo Finance) and BUIDL (BlackRock) serve Qualified Purchasers with transfer restrictions. These function primarily as representations or wrappers of traditional fund structures.
* **Quasi-permissionless models**: Products like USDY (Ondo Finance) enable freely transferable tokens for non-U.S. investors, combining broad accessibility with institutional-grade protections. Users can acquire tokens peer-to-peer without platform onboarding and use them in DeFi protocols.
* **Geographic restrictions**: Many tokenized treasuries exclude jurisdictions such as the U.S., though some (like OUSG) serve qualified U.S. investors through compliant structures.

## **Real-World Tokenized Treasury Applications**

The practical utility of tokenized Treasuries extends across multiple contexts.

Capital efficiency for active traders represents one of the clearest use cases. Rather than holding idle stablecoins between trading opportunities, crypto market participants can park capital in Treasury tokens, earning risk-free rate returns on sideline funds. When opportunities arise, tokens can be redeemed or converted to stablecoins.

DeFi collateral applications enable sophisticated strategies. Platforms like Flux Finance allow users to deposit Treasury tokens as collateral while continuing to earn the underlying yield, then borrow stablecoins against that position. The Treasury token simultaneously serves as both yield-generating and productive collateral.

Global savings access proves particularly valuable in emerging markets. Users facing domestic currency devaluation or limited local banking options can hold high-quality dollar-denominated assets earning government-backed yields, all without needing relationships with U.S. financial institutions.

These applications demonstrate how tokenized Treasuries function as both standalone investments and as components in more complex financial solutions.

## Case Study: Implementing Quasi-Permissionless Tokenized Treasuries

When Ondo Finance launched USDY in August 2023, it represented a fundamental breakthrough: the world's first quasi-permissionless, yield-bearing tokenized note secured by U.S. Treasuries. Rather than creating another walled-garden product, USDY combined institutional-grade protections with stablecoin-like accessibility.

#### Bankruptcy-Remote Architecture

USDY represents debt issued by Ondo USDY LLC, a U.S.-domiciled special purpose vehicle with an independent director. The SPV is contractually restricted to holding bank deposits and short-term U.S. Treasuries, with no employees or unrelated liabilities.

#### Quality Collateral With Equity Buffer

Senior debt secured by U.S. bank demand deposits and short-term U.S. Treasuries, with approximately 3% overcollateralization funded partially by Ondo Finance, providing alignment and a buffer against price fluctuations.

#### Third-Party Oversight

Ankura Trust serves as both Verification Agent (providing daily transparency reports) and Security Agent (with authority to seize and distribute assets to tokenholders in default scenarios).

#### Quasi-Permissionless Transferability

USDY becomes transferable after initial issuance, enabling peer-to-peer movement and DeFi composability.

#### Token Choice

USDY (accumulating) maintains constant token count while price increases. rUSDY (rebasing) maintains constant price while token balance grows, serving different user and integration needs.

#### Multi-Chain Access

Deployed across Ethereum, Mantle, Solana, Aptos, Sui, Noble (Cosmos), Arbitrum, and Stellar, meeting users across different ecosystems rather than forcing migration to a single chain.

#### The Results

In two years, USDY achieved over $1B in TVL across 8 blockchains, with tens of thousands of holders, supported by over 70 ecosystem integrations.USDY demonstrates that robust investor protections can coexist with transferability and DeFi composability.

## **Challenges and Limitations**

Despite rapid growth and institutional validation, tokenized Treasuries face several constraints.

#### 1. Regulatory Fragmentation

Tokenized Treasuries operate as securities under existing frameworks, with requirements varying significantly by jurisdiction. Different products serve different markets with tailored compliance approaches, creating some complexity for users navigating eligibility requirements.

#### 2. Access Restrictions

Many tokenized Treasuries remain restricted to Accredited Investors or Qualified Purchasers. While quasi-permissionless implementations exist for non-U.S. investors, U.S. retail investors face limited options beyond traditional brokerage access to Treasury funds.

#### 3. Limited DeFi Integration

While growing, DeFi protocol support for tokenized Treasuries isn’t yet comparable to stablecoins. As adoption expands and infrastructure matures, this limitation is gradually easing.

#### 4. Implementation Quality Variance

The wide variance in legal structures, asset quality, and investor protections creates information asymmetry for users evaluating platforms. Unlike stablecoins, which have converged around two issuers, tokenized Treasuries show substantial structural diversity.

## **The Path Forward**

Tokenized Treasuries have moved beyond proof-of-concept to become a meaningful market segment. With over $14 billion in circulating value and participation from major financial institutions, the category has achieved critical mass.

Several forces will accelerate expansion. The GENIUS Act (enacted July 2025) explicitly prohibits compliant stablecoins from paying interest to holders, cementing Treasury tokens as the yield-bearing alternative for onchain dollar exposure. Improving infrastructure continues lowering adoption barriers. Growing DeFi protocol support, maturing custody solutions, and expanding blockchain availability all reduce friction for new participants.

Market projections indicate substantial growth potential. McKinsey [estimates](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/financial-services/our-insights/from-ripples-to-waves-the-transformational-power-of-tokenizing-assets) $2 trillion in tokenized securities by 2030, with Boston Consulting Group [projecting](https://www.ledgerinsights.com/bcg-ripple-predict-tokenization-to-reach-18-9-trillion-by-2033) $18.9 trillion across all real-world assets by 2033. Even modest penetration of the $28 trillion U.S. Treasury market represents transformational growth for tokenization platforms.
