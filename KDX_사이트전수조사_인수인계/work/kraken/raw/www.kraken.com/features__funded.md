## FAQs

What is Kraken Funded?

Kraken Funded lets you trade with Kraken’s capital instead of your own. Pay a one-time challenge fee, take a trading challenge, and if you pass, you keep trading that same capital and can withdraw once you pass the minimum profit amount of $20 in profit.

How much does it cost?

One fee per challenge: $20 for the $1K plan, $90 for $10K, or $250 for $25K. The fee is non-refundable.

Beyond that there are no trading commissions during the challenge or in the funded account, and no withdrawal fees. The only other cost is the spread, the small difference between the buy and sell price, which is built into each trade in both the challenge and the funded account.

What do I need to do to pass?

Grow the starting balance by 12%. On the $1K plan, that means reaching $1,120. There’s no time limit and no minimum number of trades. The challenge ends the moment you hit the target.

What are the risks?

The only money you risk is the challenge fee. Your main Kraken balance stays untouched. This isn’t borrowing. There’s no loan, no interest, and your personal assets are never at risk. Your downside is capped at the fee, and there’s no cap on how much you can withdraw over time. The fee is non-refundable.

What if I don’t pass?

The challenge ends if the balance drops more than 3% from the starting balance. You lose nothing beyond the challenge fee since the balance was Kraken’s capital, not yours. You can start a new challenge at any tier whenever you’re ready.

How do I get paid?

Withdraw profits from the funded account any time. Sell any open positions back to USD first and make withdrawals in USD. 80% of the profits transfers to your main Kraken account; Kraken keeps 20%. Most withdrawals arrive within 24 hours. Profits you’ve already withdrawn are yours to keep, even if the funded account later closes.

What can I trade?

Kraken Funded supports 50+ major cryptocurrencies, including Bitcoin, Ethereum, and other actively traded assets. The list is more curated than what’s available across your Kraken account. You’ll see exactly which assets are available inside the Kraken Funded tab.

It’s been built so buying and selling feels exactly like trading anywhere else on Kraken. The asset list may vary slightly by region due to local availability rules.

Is this separate from my main account?

Completely. Nothing you do inside Kraken Funded affects your main balance. The capital in the challenge is Kraken’s, not yours. The only things that touch your main account are the challenge fee and profit withdrawals. Kraken Funded is operated by Payward Oceanic Ltd. (BVI).

**Funded-Trader Terms**  
If you pass the evaluation phase and become a funded trader ("FT") with POL, all market-facing transactions, if any, are carried out exclusively by POL, for POL’s own principal account and at its sole discretion. POL has discretion to determine which order type (A or B-book) it will apply. FTs do not own any trading account or position, and hold no beneficial or proprietary interest in POL’s accounts, assets or trades. When an FT submits a trade idea, POL may, in its absolute discretion, either (i) record the idea as an internal, administrative book entry and calculate a hypothetical result without routing any order externally, or (ii) accept the idea for POL’s proprietary book and route the transaction to a market maker or exchange. FTs have no control over, or visibility into, the method POL selects.

**Conflicts of Interest**  
When a funded trader’s balance hits the max-loss line, POL is released from any liability to pay future performance fees — so POL’s financial interest is opposite to yours. POL may receive financial incentives from third parties based on trade ideas provided by FTs. Any such revenue is retained solely by POL and is not shared with FTs. Because such financial incentives are not included in PnL for FTs, conflicts of interest may exist between POL and each FT. FTs should carefully consider these conflicts before participating. In addition, because Breakout earns fees each time an evaluation trader fails and then re-purchases an evaluation, conflicts of interest may also exist between Breakout and each evaluation trader.
