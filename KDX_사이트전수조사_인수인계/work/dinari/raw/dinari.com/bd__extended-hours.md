Extended-Hours Trading Disclosure

Please read this before trading outside regular market hours

Trading outside regular U.S. market hours involves risks that do not apply during regular
hours. This document describes those risks. It is not investment advice, and Dinari
Securities does not recommend securities or advise you on your orders.

When you can trade. Regular U.S. market hours are 9:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. Eastern on
days the markets are open. Dinari Securities also accepts orders during pre-market hours
of 4:00 a.m. to 9:30 a.m. Eastern and after-hours from 4:00 p.m. to 8:00 p.m. Eastern.
Orders you place outside those sessions will be queued and eligible for execution when
the next session opens / will be rejected. Trading is not available on weekends and market
holidays.

Trading outside regular market hours involves risks that do not apply during regular
hours. Please read the following carefully before placing an order in an extended-hours
session.

Lower liquidity. Liquidity means the ability to buy or sell a security readily. There are
generally fewer buyers and sellers outside regular market hours, and fewer shares
available to trade. Your order may be executed only in part, or not at all.

Higher volatility. Volatility means the degree to which prices change. Because there is
less trading activity outside regular hours, prices can move more sharply. Your order may
be executed at a price substantially different from the last price displayed, or worse than
you expected.

Changing prices. Prices in an extended-hours session may not reflect the price of the
same security at the opening of the next regular session, or at any other time. A price you
see outside regular hours may differ significantly from the price available when regular
trading resumes.

Unlinked markets. Outside regular hours, different trading venues may not be connected
to one another. Prices displayed on one venue may not reflect prices available on another.
You may receive a worse price than would have been available elsewhere at the same
moment.

News announcements. Companies often release earnings and other significant news
outside regular market hours. Combined with lower liquidity and higher volatility, this can
cause exaggerated and unsustainable price movements. A price reached in an extended-
hours session may not hold when regular trading resumes.

Wider spreads. The spread is the difference between the price at which a security can be
bought and the price at which it can be sold. Lower liquidity and higher volatility outside
regular hours can widen spreads, increasing the cost of trading.

Exchange-traded funds: index and intraday indicative values. For exchange-traded
funds and similar products, the value of the underlying index and the intraday indicative
value may not be calculated or published outside regular market hours. You may not be
able to judge whether an ETF's price reflects the value of the securities it holds, and the
price you pay or receive may differ materially from that value.

Market data outside regular hours. Prices, volume and other market data displayed on
the Platform outside regular market hours may be limited, delayed or drawn from fewer
sources than during regular hours.

Halts and suspensions. If trading in a security is halted or suspended on its primary listing
exchange, Dinari Securities will not accept or execute orders in that security until trading
resumes.

Order estimates. Any quantity or price shown to you when you enter an order is an
estimate based on conditions at that moment. The price and quantity at which your order
is actually executed may differ, and the difference can be larger outside regular market
hours.

Your positions and dShares™. Dividends, corporate actions and the recording of your
positions as dShares™ are handled the same way regardless of when your order was
executed.
