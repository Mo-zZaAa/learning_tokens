Frequently asked questions
How do we test crypto trading before going live?
Payward Services provides a separate sandbox where you build and test with test balances added through simulated deposits, using selected test networks instead of real funds. Sandbox and production are fully separate: accounts, API keys, balances and webhooks do not carry over. Production uses real funds and API keys created after Payward enables production access, and both environments restrict access to allowlisted IP addresses. See the environments documentation.
Which assets and regions can our users trade?
Coverage includes major cryptocurrencies, stablecoins, tokenized equities and a wider selection of network-native tokens. Availability varies by jurisdiction and by product, and some assets are subject to regulatory review. Order book trading focuses on high-liquidity major pairs, while onchain quotes cover pairs such as tokenized stocks and USDC. Your integration engineer can confirm availability for your markets. See the coverage documentation.
Who is Crypto Trading built for, and how do trades execute?
It is most commonly adopted by banks, brokerages and fintechs that want crypto trading inside their existing product. Trades execute through onchain quote execution or an order book, with custody and settlement handled by Payward Services. You control the user interface and branding. The documentation also covers portfolio, recurring purchase and earn use cases.
How do we verify our users before they trade?
Payward Services can run identity verification for your users. You can use Hosted KYC, where Payward collects evidence in a hosted interface, or partner-managed verification. Before requesting a quote for a new user, check that their initial onboarding is complete. See the use cases.
Has a regulated bank already launched crypto trading this way?
Yes. bunq, a European neobank with more than 20 million users across the EEA, offers in-app crypto buying, holding and selling through Payward Services. Its case study cites 640+ assets, 130+ networks and a T+1 settlement basis. Results depend on each partner's licences and markets, so talk to the team about your own scope. Read the bunq case study.
