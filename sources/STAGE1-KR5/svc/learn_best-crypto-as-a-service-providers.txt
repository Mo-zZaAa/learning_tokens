Best crypto-as-a-service providers: what to compare before you choose
Crypto
November 12, 2026
4 min read
By Payward Services editorial team
Crypto-as-a-service (CaaS) lets a bank, broker or fintech add crypto trading, custody and payments through one provider’s API. The right provider depends on which layers you need: licensing, custody, trading, fiat ramps and stablecoins. This guide lists the criteria buyers compare and shows where Payward Services Crypto Trading fits: execution, custody and settlement across more than 600 assets where available, through one integration, with partners typically live in six to 12 weeks from contract signature. Asset availability varies by jurisdiction and product.
What crypto-as-a-service includes
CaaS delivers crypto capabilities as APIs: wallets, custody, trading and compliance tooling. The provider runs the back end. You own the interface and the customer relationship.
Providers package these layers differently. Some cover the whole stack, while others cover one layer, such as custody or wallets. Knowing which layers you need is the first step in building a shortlist.
Criteria to compare providers
Write down your answer to each criterion before you talk to vendors, then ask every provider the same questions and get the answers in writing.
Regulatory scope and contracting entity. Which entity do you contract with, which licenses does it hold, and in which markets?
Custody model. Who holds the keys and the customer assets, and how are they segregated and audited?
Trading and liquidity. Where are orders executed, how are quotes set, and what sits between you and the price source?
Fiat on and off ramps. Which payment methods and jurisdictions are supported, and are ramps part of the same contract?
Compliance duties. Who runs KYC, AML, sanctions screening and the Travel Rule, and what is left for you to build?
Time to launch. How long from contract to live customers, including compliance review, not only the API integration?
Assets and networks. Which assets and networks are supported today, and how are new ones added?
Pricing and revenue model. Can you earn through spread, fees or maker-taker pricing, and what does the provider charge?
Exit options. What happens to customer assets and data if you change provider?
Types of provider
Providers tend to fall into four groups. Individual vendors often straddle more than one, so check each against the criteria above.
Brokerage and stablecoin APIs. Focused on buying, selling and moving value, often with stablecoin payments.
Custody-led platforms. Start from safekeeping and add trading or staking on top.
Wallet infrastructure. Provides wallets and key management, with execution and liquidity sourced separately.
Full-stack, exchange-backed providers. Combine execution, custody and settlement behind one integration.
Buyers commonly shortlist providers such as Coinbase, BitGo, Fireblocks, Zero Hash and Paxos. Review each provider’s own documentation for current scope, since offerings change.

Which provider type fits which business
Bank: prioritize custody model, audit and segregation, and regulatory scope. A single provider for execution, custody and settlement can reduce vendor count.
Fintech or neobank: prioritize time to launch, ramps and payment-method coverage across your target markets.
Broker: prioritize liquidity, quote mechanics, asset coverage and revenue model.
Payments firm: prioritize ramps, stablecoin support and settlement speed.
Frequently asked questions
What is crypto-as-a-service?
Crypto-as-a-service lets a bank, broker or fintech add crypto trading, custody and payments through one provider’s API. The provider runs the back end, and you own the interface and the customer.
How long does launch take?

Who holds the assets?
With Payward Services Crypto Trading, execution, custody and settlement are handled by Payward Services. For banks, custody is segregated and audited. Always confirm the custody model in writing with any provider.
