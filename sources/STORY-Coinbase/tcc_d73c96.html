CFTC Approves Coinbase Clearing as Derivatives Clearing Organization

Published September 28, 2026
Updated October 17, 2026

By

  • Coinbase Clearing gains CFTC approval to clear fully collateralized futures, options on futures, and swaps.
  • The approval completes Coinbase's CFTC-regulated derivatives stack spanning a futures commission merchant and a designated contract market.
  • Coinbase Clearing is built with USDC collateral and round-the-clock settlement to speed regulated product launches.

The Commodity Futures Trading Commission approved the registration of Coinbase Clearing LLC as a derivatives clearing organization on September 28, 2026, permitting the company to clear fully collateralized futures, options on futures, and swaps.

The Order of Registration was issued in Washington, D.C., and signed by Christopher J. Kirkpatrick, Secretary of the Commission. Coinbase Clearing submitted its application under Section 5b of the Commodity Exchange Act, 7 U.S.C. § 7a-1. The Commission found that the application, including all amendments and representations, demonstrated compliance with the applicable requirements of the Act and the Commission’s regulations for registration as a DCO, as required by Section 5b and Commission Regulation 39.3(a), 17 C.F.R. § 39.3(a), and that it provided sufficient assurance of continued compliance.

Under the order’s first condition, Coinbase Clearing is permitted to clear, in its capacity as a DCO, fully collateralized futures, options on futures, and swaps. A position cleared by the company is fully collateralized if it meets the definition of “fully collateralized position” in Commission Regulation 39.2. The CFTC’s derivatives clearing organization filing record lists Coinbase Clearing LLC with the status “Registered,” dated September 28, 2026, with the remark: “Registered by Commission order; permitted to clear fully collateralized futures, options on futures, and swaps.”

Two further conditions govern the registration. Should the Commission promulgate or amend a regulation addressing or otherwise affecting any aspect of the order, that regulation will apply and supersede the applicable terms of the order. The Commission also reserved the right, for any reason in its own discretion, to condition, modify, suspend, terminate, or otherwise restrict the order’s terms on its own motion, as appropriate and as permitted by law. The order states that it is based on the representations and supporting material provided by the company, and that any changes to or omissions in the material facts or circumstances underlying it may require a new or amended order.

The order recites several representations by the company. Coinbase Clearing represented that all funds held in any clearing member account will be considered member property, as that term is defined in the U.S. Bankruptcy Code, and that it will at all times maintain the funds of its clearing members separate and distinct from its own. It also represented that it will ensure the performance of all self-regulatory functions required of a registered DCO, including monitoring and enforcing clearing member compliance with its admission and continuing eligibility standards and enforcing compliance with its other rules, regulations, and procedures. Commission staff reviewed and analyzed the application, the company’s rules, all amendments, and its representations, and conducted a technical evaluation of the proposed operations against each DCO Core Principle and corresponding regulation in accordance with Section 5b(c)(2)(A)(i) of the Act.

Coinbase’s Announcement

Coinbase announced the approval the same day, describing Coinbase Clearing as the first USDC-native clearinghouse and saying the registration completes its full stack of CFTC-regulated derivatives infrastructure: a futures commission merchant, Coinbase Financial Markets, Inc.; a designated contract market, Coinbase Derivatives, LLC; and now a registered DCO.

According to the company, Coinbase Clearing is built with USDC collateral and 24/7 settlement, and Coinbase can for the first time create and settle fully collateralized contracts directly. It characterized the capability as enabling faster product development, more efficient operations, and the flexibility to bring new regulated products to market over time. Coinbase said it will continue to use existing partners to support certain products, including its margined derivatives business and its upcoming launch of single stock perps.

“Today’s CFTC approval completes Coinbase’s end-to-end derivatives infrastructure, enabling us to bring more regulated derivatives products to market with native USDC collateral and 24/7 settlement,” said Molly Abraham, General Counsel, Coinbase.

The company framed the approval as incremental progress on its commitment to expanding access to regulated products and to building the financial infrastructure for the onchain economy, and said it will look to strengthen the infrastructure supporting its current products and build a scalable foundation for the future.

The Filed Application

In the Exhibit A-3 narrative filed with its application, Coinbase Clearing said it would provide clearing services for contracts traded on designated contract markets and swap execution facilities that agree to abide by its rules, procedures, and risk management requirements, and that it anticipated providing clearing services upon registration. The company said it will offer clearing and settlement services directly to market participants as well as to customers of FCMs that become FCM clearing members. All products cleared through the company are required to be fully collateralized, which the exhibit said negates the need to calculate variation margin levels or maintain a default fund and makes it easier to offer clearing services directly to participants.

The Form DCO cover sheet lists Coinbase Clearing LLC’s principal executive offices, and the principal office where its clearing activities will be conducted, at One Madison Avenue, New York, NY 10010. In Exhibit A-7, the company stated it is applying to do business in the United States and has no other applications or exemption requests pending or denied in any other jurisdiction; its address for legal service of process is Corporation Service Company, 251 Little Falls Drive, Wilmington, DE 19808. The filing record’s associated documents also include a regulatory compliance chart (Exhibit A-1), a proposed rulebook (Exhibit A-2), a certificate of good standing (Exhibit A-8), and a certificate of formation (Exhibit A-8a).
