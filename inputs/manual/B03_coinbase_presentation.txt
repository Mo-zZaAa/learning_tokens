Alesia Haas, CFO: Thanks, Brian. All right, let's get started with our financial results. In the 3rd quarter of
2026, we generated $1.2 billion of total revenues, split roughly half and half between transaction revenues and
subscription and services revenues. Quarterly net income was $719 million, driven largely by unrealized gains
on crypto assets that we hold for investment. Our adjusted net income was $78 million, and we grew adjusted
EBITDA 60% quarter over quarter to $333 million. I will unpack these results and more in the following slides.

But let's start with a quick read on the quarter. The market backdrop was challenging, but the business is
healthy. As we go through the slides today, all the comparisons I'm going to share will be on a
quarter-over-quarter basis unless I note otherwise.

We started the quarter with year-to-date lows in crypto market volumes and volatility. Total market crypto
trading volumes fell 10% in the quarter, and volatility compressed to multi-year lows. However, conditions did
improve in September, and total crypto market cap rose 37% from the end of Q2, and volatility increased

through the end of the quarter. Against that backdrop, Coinbase gained crypto trading volume market share for
the 4th consecutive quarter across both spot and derivatives trading volumes.

Prediction markets continued to scale. We surpassed $200 million in annualized revenues. We are pleased to
see more than a 60% increase in daily prediction traders. And these traders, they're trading a wide variety of
contracts. In the 3rd quarter, more than 50% of contracts traded were non-sports.

Our subscription and services revenues grew 3% thanks to strength in stablecoins, staking, and financing.
Coinbase One paid subscribers increased for the 5th quarter in a row. And we lowered our adjusted expenses.

Our results were in line or lower than our guidance. Subscription and services trended towards the high end of
the range, thanks to strong native unit inflows across USDC, staking, and custody. And we saw a modest
tailwind from asset prices that rose late in the quarter, as I mentioned.

Transaction expenses were within our mid-teens outlook, though slightly elevated due to the strong growth in
prediction markets. Adjusted expenses came in slightly lower than our outlook. The largest driver of the decline
in Q3 was first, lower headcount, as we received a full quarter of benefit of the reductions we announced in the
second quarter. Second, we saw lower professional services expenses, which in part was driven by spend
shifting from the 3rd quarter to the 4th quarter.

Total revenue was $1.2 billion in Q3, roughly flat despite a 10% decline in total market crypto trading volume I
mentioned earlier. The growth in prediction markets and subscription and services helped offset the softer
crypto trading activity. Predictions and derivatives combined contributed 25% of transaction revenue, and
subscription and services contributed nearly half of net revenue. Our focus is on building a diversified portfolio
of products that reduces our reliance on crypto market cycles and supports durable long-term revenue growth.

Transaction revenue was essentially stable at $590 million. Consumer revenue was $450 million, roughly flat,
as continued growth in prediction markets helped offset softer crypto trading activity. Institutional revenue was
$92 million, down 8%, roughly in line with broader market declines, and other transaction revenue was $48
million, up 2%.

Subscription and services revenue grew 3% to $570 million, representing 49% of net revenue. Stablecoin
revenue grew 5% to $307 million. The average USDC held in Coinbase products reached a new all-time high
of $22 billion, and higher balances and interest rates helped more than offset the lower off-platform balances.
Blockchain rewards grew 3% to $86 million, driven by higher asset prices and growth in native units staked.
Interest and finance fee income grew 4% to $69 million, supported by a new all-time high in our average daily
loans outstanding. Other subscription and services revenue was $109 million, down 4%.

Now turning to expenses. Adjusted expenses were $975 million, down 6%. Technology and development
declined 5% to $447 million, reflecting lower employee-related spend and a full quarter benefit from the actions
we announced in May. General and administrative expenses declined 11% to $319 million, primarily from lower
professional services expenses, due in part to the anticipated Q3 expenses shifting to the 4th quarter. Sales
and marketing expenses increased 1% to $243 million as higher USDC rewards were partially offset by lower
media and event spend. Transaction expenses were 17% of net revenue, with growth in prediction markets
increasing the contribution from associated exchange fees.
